SON V2 - PRE-SPECIFIED PROTOCOL (new round, written BEFORE any V2 run)
Date: 2026-10-18
Status: independent new experiment, started after the 600-run confirmatory
result. The earlier 600 runs are NOT reused as evidence for V2 hypotheses.

QUESTION
In OLSRv1, does the neighbor-hold time (not the HELLO sending rate) determine
how quickly departed neighbors are detected, and can a shorter hold factor with
a slower HELLO rate keep PDR close to default OLSR with fewer control bytes?

MECHANISM BEING TESTED (from source reading; not yet verified by trace)
A receiver expires a neighbor using the validity time carried by that neighbor's
last HELLO. Changing the sender's rate after the neighbor has left cannot shorten
that timer. Detection speed is therefore governed by hold time.

POLICIES (all static; hold = factor x hello)
  olsr_default = h2_k3  : HELLO 2 s, hold 6 s    (stock reference)
  olsr_slow    = h5_k3  : HELLO 5 s, hold 15 s   (stock reference)
  h3_k3                 : HELLO 3 s, hold 9 s    (stock; fixed-rate frontier point)
  h3_k2.5               : HELLO 3 s, hold 7.5 s  (patched)
  h3_k2                 : HELLO 3 s, hold 6 s    (patched; same hold as default)
  h5_k2.5               : HELLO 5 s, hold 12.5 s (patched)

DESIGN
  6 policies x 5 scenarios (stable, fading, churn, outage, stress)
  x 3 sizes (25, 50, 100) x 10 NEW seeds
  (2003, 2011, 2017, 2027, 2029, 2039, 2053, 2063, 2069, 2081) = 900 runs.
  One process per run, fixed AssignStreams, identical traffic and topology schedule.
  Code: son_study2.cc (frozen before the run; any later change = new round).

HYPOTHESES (to test, not observed facts)
  H1: stale_sym_entry_s and stale_clear_lag_s depend mainly on hold time, not on
      HELLO interval, across h2_k3, h3_k2, h5_k2.5, h5_k3.
  H2: h3_k2 (same 6 s hold as default) has lower olsr_bytes_per_node_min than
      olsr_default with a PDR difference of at most 2 percentage points in churn
      and outage. (This may fail: a hold factor of 2 tolerates only one lost HELLO.)
  H3: any benefit is conditional on scenario; no universal claim.

PRIMARY OUTCOMES (stratified by scenario and node count)
  pdr_percent and olsr_bytes_per_node_min, difference vs olsr_default,
  seed-block bootstrap 95% CI over the 10 seeds.
SECONDARY
  pdr_before/during/after, stale_sym_entry_s, missing_sym_entry_s (non-fading only),
  stale_clear_lag_s, missing_clear_lag_s, socket_errors.
  Lag value -1 means "not applicable or not cleared before the window ended";
  never average -1.

RULES
  1. Report all 900 runs, including invalid rows (valid=0), failed runs and null results.
  2. No parameter change after seeing V2 results. A change creates a new round with new seeds.
  3. Multiple comparisons are marked exploratory unless corrected.
  4. A negative or null result is reported as such.
  5. Known limits: simulation only; OLSRv1 stock model plus one patched attribute;
     control bytes are OLSR packet bytes, not MAC/PHY airtime or energy;
     one grid topology; five flows; node 0 is isolated in outage/stress for every policy.
  6. Novelty: adaptive/tuned HELLO and hold parameters exist in the OLSR literature.
     Any contribution is the controlled separation of rate vs hold time with
     stale-state measurements, not HELLO tuning itself.
